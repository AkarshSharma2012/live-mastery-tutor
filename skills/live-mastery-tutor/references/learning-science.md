# Learning-science rationale

The protocol emphasizes methods with strong support and keeps claims proportional to the evidence.

## Applied principles

- **Retrieval practice:** recalling knowledge strengthens later access better than passive rereading alone.
- **Spacing:** successful retrievals separated in time support durable retention; the exact ideal interval depends on the goal and learner.
- **Interleaving:** mixing already introduced concepts can improve discrimination and transfer, but it should not precede basic instruction.
- **Elaboration and teach-back:** explaining why and connecting ideas exposes gaps hidden by recognition.
- **Concrete examples plus boundaries:** examples build an initial model; counterexamples prevent overgeneralization.
- **Mastery gates:** instruction is not evidence. The tutor separates exposure, immediate performance, practical execution, and delayed retention.
- **Calibration:** confidence paired with performance helps detect guessing and false fluency.

## Primary and review sources

- Weinstein, Madan, and Sumeracki, “Teaching the science of learning,” 2018: https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-017-0087-y
- Sana and Yan, “Interleaving Retrieval Practice Promotes Science Learning,” 2022: https://doi.org/10.1177/09567976211057507
- Karpicke and Bauernschmidt, “Spaced Retrieval,” 2011: https://doi.org/10.1037/a0023437

These sources support general design choices, not a claim that one fixed schedule is optimal for every learner or subject.
